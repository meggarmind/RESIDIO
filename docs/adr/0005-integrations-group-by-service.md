# Integrations group by the service connected, not by the subject it serves

Settings groups by subject/module, matching the dimension the main dashboard sidebar already uses. Integrations is the single stated exception: WhatsApp, Gmail import, and later Paystack and SMS are grouped together because they are external services, even though the subjects they serve — communications, billing imports, payments — are spread across other groups.

Applied strictly, the subject rule would file WhatsApp under Communications and the Gmail import connection under Billing & Finance, which is exactly where they sit today and exactly what prompted this work. That arrangement is defensible in the abstract and unusable in practice. An administrator whose Twilio credentials have expired is not thinking about communications; they are thinking about a service that has stopped working. The thing being configured is the connection, and connections resemble each other far more than the subjects behind them do. Grouping them together also gives Paystack and SMS an obvious home — both are integrations today with no settings page at all, and under a pure subject rule each arrival would be another judgement call.

An Integration is also exempt from the configuration-only boundary of ADR-0004. Its page owns credentials, configuration and operational console together: the WhatsApp page keeps its opt-in registry, pending-contact triage, live sessions and session reset alongside its provider credentials and rollout controls, and the Gmail import page keeps connect, disconnect and sync status even though it configures no rule whatsoever. This is not a weakening of ADR-0004 but a consequence of what an Integration is. Connecting a service persists a credential that governs all future behaviour, which is configuration, while the same page must show whether the connection is alive, which is monitoring. Splitting them would leave two half-pages, and in Gmail import's case would leave an empty one. An Integration is something you connect, tune and watch in one place.

The vocabulary matters here and is recorded in `CONTEXT.md`. An **Integration** is the external service; a **Provider** is the vendor implementing one, so Meta and Twilio are two Providers of the WhatsApp Integration. This preserves the existing meaning of "provider" established in ADR-0003 and in `src/lib/whatsapp/providers/`, rather than overloading it.

The cost is that WhatsApp no longer appears under Communications, where someone reasoning purely by subject would look for it, and that the grouping rule now has an exception a maintainer must know about. This ADR exists precisely because a future reader who knows the rule but not the exception will otherwise "correct" WhatsApp back under Communications and reintroduce the scatter.
