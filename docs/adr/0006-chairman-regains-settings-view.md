# Chairman regains settings.view, because the Settings module was never actually closed to them

Migration `20260830100200_chairman_excludes_settings_module.sql` removed every permission in the `settings` and `system` categories from the Chairman role, on the product definition that Chairman is the super administrator without the Settings module. This decision restores `settings.view` alone.

The reason is that the exclusion did not achieve what it claimed. Chairman still holds nine permissions that gate individual Settings pages — `announcements.manage_categories`, `announcements.manage_templates`, `documents.manage_categories`, `billing.manage_profiles`, `email_imports.view`, `email_imports.configure`, `notifications.manage`, `security.manage_categories` and `whatsapp.view` — because those permissions live in other categories and the migration only deleted `settings` and `system`. Middleware resolves the longest matching route prefix, so `/settings/message-templates` is checked against its own entry and admitted, while `/settings` itself is checked against `settings.view` and refused. The practical result is that a Chairman can reach eight or so Settings pages by deep link and see a working sidebar, but is bounced from the section's front door. That is not a permission boundary; it is a missing landing page.

Two ways out were available. Revoking the other nine permissions would have made the original claim true, but it would also take announcement templates, document categories and billing profiles away from a role that plausibly uses them day to day — a substantive product change smuggled in as a consistency fix. Granting `settings.view` instead makes the section coherent without widening access by a single page: what a Chairman sees inside Settings is still decided entirely by the nine permissions they already hold.

The boundary is therefore now stated positively rather than negatively. Chairman does not hold the `settings.manage_*` permissions, nor anything in the `system` category, so estate configuration, billing rules, security settings, audit logs, role management and maintenance remain closed to them. What they hold is access to the reference data and communications surfaces their role already administers, reachable through the front door rather than only by URL.

Two documents asserted the stronger claim and were wrong before this change, not because of it: `website/docs/settings/settings-overview.md` stated that "the Chairman role has no access to Settings at all", and `src/lib/audit/README.md` described audit logs as viewable by chairman. Both are corrected alongside the migration.
